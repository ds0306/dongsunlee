About

Dong Sun Lee is a professor in the Department of Political Science and International Relations at Korea University. He received his Ph.D. in political science from the University of Chicago and has held academic appointments, including as a visiting scholar at George Washington University in 2010 and a research fellow at the East-West Center from 2004 to 2007. Dr. Lee’s research focuses on Asian security and international relations theory, with a particular interest in asymmetric international politics.

Dr. Lee is the author of Nuclear Alliance Restraint: Success and Failure in Countering Allied Proliferators (Routledge) and Power Shifts, Strategy, and War: Declining States and International Conflict (Routledge). His articles have been published in scholarly journals such as Asian Security, Australian Journal of International Affairs, British Journal of Politics and International Relations, Contemporary Security Policy, Diplomacy and Statecraft, International Relations of the Asia-Pacific, Journal of East Asian Studies, Korean Journal of Defense Analysis, Korean Journal of International Studies, Korea Observer, and Pacific Focus. Dr. Lee has also contributed chapters to edited volumes, including The Long Shadow: Nuclear Weapons and Security in 21st Century Asia (Stanford University Press) and The International Encyclopedia of Peace (Oxford University Press).

Dr. Lee has had the privilege of serving on policy advisory boards for the Republic of Korea’s Ministries of Unification, Foreign Affairs, and National Defense, contributing to policy discussions at national levels.
